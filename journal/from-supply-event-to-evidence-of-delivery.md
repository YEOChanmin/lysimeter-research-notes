## My question

What can I reasonably conclude when the notebook says that a supply event occurred?

For my lysimeter research, the answer depends on the evidence behind that sentence. Device reports, field observations and delivered-volume measurements answer different questions. I want those distinctions to remain visible when interpreting the records.

## What the public record says

The [field-revision entry](2026-10-19-field-revision.md#what-was-supported-by-the-records) summarises observed nutrient inflow and stopping, alongside device reports. It leaves measured volume for every pulse and long-term unattended reliability unestablished.

The same entry describes a mass disturbance associated with automatic water supply and a subsequent hold. The event remains in the ledger with its intervention context. Preserving it does not make the surrounding measurements undisturbed research observations.

These are summaries of previously published records. I did not repeat those observations while preparing this note.

## Different evidence, different conclusions

| Evidence available | Conclusion it can support | Question still open |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Device acknowledgment | The device reports acceptance of an action | Did physical supply occur? |
| Device completion or OFF report | The device reports its action or state | Was inflow or stopping physically observed? |
| Field observation | Inflow or stopping was observed under the recorded conditions | What quantity was delivered, and what happens under other conditions? |
| Duration multiplied by nominal flow | An accounted volume under the stated assumption | Does it agree with directly measured delivery? |
| Direct measurement of delivery | A quantity under its documented method and conditions | What uncertainty and applicability limits accompany that measurement? |
| Ledger record or backup comparison | An event is recorded, or selected files match within the comparison scope | Is the record complete for the intended period and data types? |

The measurement row describes an evidence category, not a new result from this system. These distinctions follow the public [evidence guide](../methods/evidence-and-limitations.md) and [measurement notes](../methods/measurement-and-quality.md).

## How I would write the conclusion

For this note, I propose a writing rule: name the evidence, such as “the device reported completion” or “field observation recorded inflow and stopping.” Volume from nominal flow and duration should remain labelled as accounted volume, with its assumption visible.

Backup evidence also needs its own description. A successful comparison of selected records does not establish a complete full-day backup. A stored supply event does not itself establish physical delivery.

This is a writing and interpretation principle. It does not demonstrate that every current software path enforces these distinctions.

## Limitations and next questions

This note adds no numerical analysis, new experimental evidence or current-operation claim. Public summaries alone cannot establish per-pulse delivered volume or complete records.

- What evidence is sufficient to connect a device report, a physical observation and a stored event to the same operation?
- How should an intervention remain visible when a later analysis selects comparable measurement intervals?
- What additional evidence would justify replacing an accounted-volume statement with a measured-delivery statement?
